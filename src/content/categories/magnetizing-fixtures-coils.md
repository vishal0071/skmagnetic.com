### Coil, fixture or magnetizing yoke?

The names overlap in everyday use. A coil is a plain solenoid that magnetizes along its axis. A fixture is built around one part to give a radial or multi-pole pattern. A yoke adds steel pole pieces that carry the field to the magnet, which is useful for thin, flat parts or for poles on one face only. Which one suits your part depends on the magnetization direction you need — our guide to [radial, axial and diametrical magnetization](/blog/radial-axial-diametrical-magnetization/) explains the differences.

### What decides fixture performance

- **Conductor size and turns** — set the field strength and the current the fixture must carry.
- **Pole geometry** — sets how sharply and accurately the poles are formed.
- **Mechanical strength** — the pulse creates large forces inside the fixture, so it must be well supported.
- **Cooling** — natural, forced-air or water cooling determines how fast you can cycle.
