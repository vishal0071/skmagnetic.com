## Why magnets lose strength

Permanent magnets are stable, but some conditions cause a loss that does not recover by itself:

- **Heat:** running a magnet above its rated temperature causes losses that remain after it cools. NdFeB grades vary widely in temperature rating.
- **Opposing fields:** high current in a motor, for example during a stall or overload, can partly demagnetize the magnets. Ferrite also becomes easier to demagnetize at low temperatures.
- **Disassembly:** alnico magnets in particular lose strength when removed from their magnetic circuit, which is why older speakers and magnetos often need re-magnetizing after rework.
- **Handling and storage:** magnets stored next to opposing magnets, or exposed to strong stray fields, can be partly demagnetized.

In these cases the material itself is unchanged, so the loss can usually be recovered by re-magnetizing.

## What can be recovered, and what cannot

Re-magnetizing restores magnetization, not material. Magnets that are corroded (a common problem with NdFeB whose coating is damaged), cracked or chipped, or that have been heated far enough to change their structure, may not recover fully however strong the pulse. In those cases, replacing the magnet is the reliable fix.

## How re-magnetizing is done

The process is the same as first-time magnetizing, using a [magnet charging machine](/products/magnetizing-machines/magnet-charging-machine/) with the right coil or fixture:

- **Loose magnets** are recharged in a [magnetizing coil](/products/magnetizing-fixtures-coils/magnetizing-coil/), in the same direction and polarity as before.
- **Rotors and housings** need a fixture that matches the original pole count, with the part aligned so the new poles land exactly where the old ones were.
- **Loudspeakers** are recharged as a complete assembly, just as in [speaker production](/industries/loudspeakers-audio/).

Recharging a weak magnet still needs the full saturation field: roughly 1–1.5 T for ferrite, 2.5–3.5 T for sintered NdFeB and 3.5–5 T for SmCo. Check the result against a known good part with a gauss meter or fluxmeter.

Rework areas are often less controlled than production lines, so make sure operators follow the guidance in our article on [magnetizer safety and maintenance](/blog/magnetizer-safety-maintenance/).
