## When loose magnet charging is used

- **Magnet makers and traders** charging stock before dispatch.
- **Assembly plants** that fit pre-magnetized magnets, for example some interior-magnet rotor designs where magnets are charged before insertion.
- **Sensor and switch makers** using small block, disc and ring magnets.
- **Testing and sampling**, where a few magnets are charged and measured to check a batch.

## How it works

The magnet sits in the bore of a [magnetizing coil](/products/magnetizing-fixtures-coils/magnetizing-coil/), a solenoid that produces a near-uniform field along its axis. A [magnet charging machine](/products/magnetizing-machines/magnet-charging-machine/) discharges its capacitor bank through the coil in a pulse lasting a few milliseconds, magnetizing the magnet through its thickness or length. For the physics behind the pulse, see [how a capacitor discharge magnetizer works](/blog/how-capacitor-discharge-magnetizer-works/).

### How much field is needed?

As a rough guide, ferrite saturates at about 1–1.5 T, sintered NdFeB at about 2.5–3.5 T and SmCo at about 3.5–5 T. A common rule of thumb is a field of about three times the magnet's intrinsic coercivity (Hcj). Size the coil and machine for the hardest material you plan to charge, not the easiest.

## Practical tips

- **Orientation:** anisotropic magnets only reach full strength along their oriented direction, so holders should keep them aligned with the coil axis.
- **Polarity:** decide which face should be north before you start, and mark or test parts consistently.
- **Handling after charging:** charged magnets attract each other and any nearby steel. Collect them in non-magnetic trays with spacers to avoid chipping and pinched fingers.
- **Checking:** a gauss meter reading on the face, or a fluxmeter with a Helmholtz coil, confirms that a batch is fully charged.

A coil magnetizes in one direction only. If your part needs several poles on one face or around its edge, it needs a shaped fixture instead; see [multi-pole magnetizing](/applications/multipole-magnetizing/).
