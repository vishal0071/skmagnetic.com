## Magnetizing for sensing applications

In a sensor, the magnet is part of the measurement. An encoder counts poles, a Hall sensor switches at a set field and a reed switch closes when the magnet comes within range. Any variation in pole position or strength shows up as variation in the signal, which is why many sensor makers prefer to magnetize in-house, where they control the process.

## Common sensor magnets

### Multi-pole encoder and speed rings

These are ring magnets, often bonded NdFeB, bonded ferrite or rubber-bonded ferrite, magnetized with many poles on the outer diameter or on one face. They are magnetized with a [multi-pole magnetizing fixture](/products/magnetizing-fixtures-coils/multipole-magnetizing-fixture/) built for the exact pole count and diameter. Our page on [multi-pole magnetizing](/applications/multipole-magnetizing/) explains how pole accuracy is controlled.

### Hall sensor and angle sensor magnets

Small discs and blocks, magnetized axially or diametrically, set the field the sensor detects. The magnet's strength and alignment decide where, and how reliably, the sensor switches.

### Reed switch and meter magnets

Simple block, rod and ring magnets used to trigger reed switches, or for coupling and sensing in meters, are usually charged in a [magnetizing coil](/products/magnetizing-fixtures-coils/magnetizing-coil/) driven by a capacitor discharge magnetizer.

## Getting repeatable results

- **Material and orientation:** isotropic magnets accept any pole pattern, while anisotropic magnets must be magnetized along the direction they were oriented in.
- **Fixture precision:** for fine pole pitches, the conductors sit close to the magnet surface and must be precisely located, and the part must be held the same way every time.
- **Verification:** magnetic viewing film shows the pole pattern at a glance, and a Hall probe scan measures pole position and field strength.

Our article on [how multi-pole fixtures create fine pole patterns](/blog/multipole-magnetizing-explained/) covers fixture design in more detail.
