## Where magnetizing fits in automotive production

Automotive and two-wheeler components are built in high volumes, on lines where handling strong magnets causes real problems: parts jumping together, chipped ferrite and steel particles stuck inside housings. Assembling the magnets unmagnetized and magnetizing the finished part removes these issues and makes automatic loading far simpler.

## Typical parts and how they are magnetized

### Starter, wiper, blower and window-lift motors

These PMDC motors generally use ferrite arc magnets fixed inside a steel housing. A [motor housing magnetizing fixture](/products/magnetizing-fixtures-coils/motor-housing-magnetizing-fixture/) fits inside the housing and magnetizes the arcs radially from the inside, usually in two or four poles. The fixture locates the housing the same way every time, because pole position affects torque and noise.

### Magneto flywheels

Two-wheeler magneto flywheels carry magnets on the inside of the flywheel cup. They are magnetized in a multi-pole pattern by a fixture placed inside the flywheel, which sets the number and position of the poles the generator is designed around.

### Sensor magnets

Speed and position sensors use small ring, disc or block magnets, often with a precise multi-pole pattern. Our page on [multi-pole magnetizing](/applications/multipole-magnetizing/) explains how pole count and accuracy are controlled.

## Building a magnetizing station

A typical station pairs a [capacitor discharge magnetizer](/products/magnetizing-machines/magnet-charging-machine/) with one fixture per part type. The pulse lasts only a few milliseconds, but at automotive volumes fixture cooling and capacitor recharge time decide the real cycle rate. Peak-current monitoring can flag any part that did not receive a full pulse, and a gauss check at each pole confirms the result.

Before specifying a station, it helps to settle the magnet material, the hardest part to magnetize, the output per shift and the checks your customer expects. Our guide on [how to choose a magnetizer](/blog/how-to-choose-magnetizer/) walks through these decisions.
