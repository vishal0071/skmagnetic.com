## How a digital gaussmeter works

The probe carries a small **Hall-effect sensor**: a thin semiconductor element with a small current flowing through it. When a magnetic field passes through the element, it produces a voltage proportional to the field. The meter amplifies that voltage and shows it as **flux density** in gauss, millitesla or tesla. Because the voltage changes sign when the field reverses, the meter also shows whether the probe is facing a **north or a south pole**.

## Transverse and axial probes

- A **transverse probe** is flat, with the sensor at its tip. It measures fields that pass through the flat face — on the surface of a magnet, between pole pieces or in a narrow air gap.
- An **axial probe** measures fields along its length, such as the field inside a solenoid or a [magnetizer coil](/products/magnetizer-fixtures-coils/).

## Getting repeatable readings

Gaussmeter readings depend on where and how the probe is held. Measure at the same point, at the same angle and at the same distance from the surface each time, ideally using a fixture. The field of a magnet also changes with temperature, so measure parts at a similar temperature when comparing batches. To compare the overall strength of whole magnets, a [digital fluxmeter](/products/test-measuring-instruments/digital-fluxmeter/) is often the better tool.
