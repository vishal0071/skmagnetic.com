## How a multi-pole magnetizing fixture works

A multi-pole fixture is a precision-machined body with slots around the surface that faces the magnet. A conductor runs through each slot, and the current direction alternates from one slot to the next. When a [capacitor discharge magnet charging machine](/products/magnetizing-machines/magnet-charging-machine/) fires through the fixture, the fields of neighbouring conductors add together in the region between them, forming a north pole at one position and a south pole at the next. The conductors sit at the pole boundaries, so the number of slots sets the number of poles — always an even number.

Because every pole is formed in the same pulse, the pattern is fixed by the fixture's machining rather than by assembling separate magnets. That makes multi-pole fixtures the standard way to magnetize one-piece ring and encoder magnets. The basics are covered in [multi-pole magnetizing explained](/blog/multipole-magnetizing-explained/).

### Radial multi-pole or simple radial?

When people ask about ring magnet radial magnetization, they usually mean one of two patterns. In a radial multi-pole ring, the magnetization runs through the ring wall in each pole, alternating outward and inward around the circumference — the pattern OD and ID fixtures produce for motor and sensor rings. In a simple radial ring, the whole outer surface is one pole and the bore is the other, which calls for a different fixture arrangement. Axial and diametrical patterns are different again; our guide to [radial, axial and diametrical magnetization](/blog/radial-axial-diametrical-magnetization/) compares all of them.

## What determines pole quality

### Pole count and pitch

More poles on the same diameter mean a narrower pole pitch and less room for each conductor. Narrow poles are also magnetized less deeply: the field from a fine-pitch fixture falls away quickly with distance, so fine patterns are concentrated near the magnet surface. Pole count, part size and magnet thickness have to be considered together at the design stage.

### Pole accuracy and part location

Pole position accuracy depends on how precisely the slots are machined, how well the part is centred and angularly located, and how uniform the magnet material is. Where pitch tolerance matters — encoders especially — it can be specified on the drawing and checked by pole scanning, in which a Hall probe measures the field as the part rotates.

### Skew

For motor rings, the poles can be skewed along the part length to reduce cogging torque. The skew angle is set by the motor designer and machined into the fixture.

### Magnet material and orientation

Isotropic bonded NdFeB and isotropic ferrite can be magnetized with almost any pole pattern. Anisotropic rings are oriented during pressing, either radially or in a polar pattern, and must be magnetized to suit that orientation. A polar-oriented ring works only with the pole count and alignment it was pressed for.
