## How a speaker magnetizer works

The motor of a loudspeaker is a small magnetic circuit: the magnet, a back plate with its centre pole piece, and a top plate. Together they concentrate flux into the narrow gap where the voice coil moves. In production these parts are glued and centred while the magnet is still unmagnetized. The finished assembly — or the complete driver — then goes into a fixture connected to a [capacitor discharge magnet charging machine](/products/magnetizing-machines/magnet-charging-machine/), and a single pulse lasting a few milliseconds magnetizes the magnet through its thickness.

The steel plates sit in the field path during the pulse, so the fixture has to be designed around the whole assembly rather than the bare magnet. In other words, a speaker magnet charging machine is really a matched pair of machine and fixture. If the principle is new to you, read [how a capacitor discharge magnetizer works](/blog/how-capacitor-discharge-magnetizer-works/).

## External and internal magnet speakers

| | External-magnet design | Internal-magnet design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical magnet | Ferrite ring | NdFeB disc or ring (occasionally ferrite) |
| Magnet position | Outside the voice coil, between back plate and top plate | Inside a steel cup, beneath the pole plate |
| Approx. field to saturate | 1–1.5 T | 2.5–3.5 T for NdFeB |
| Common products | Woofers, PA, car audio and multimedia drivers | Tweeters, compact and lightweight drivers |
| Fixture notes | Field applied through the ring magnet and its plates | Field has to be driven through the surrounding steel cup |

Because neodymium needs a much higher field, a line that runs both designs should be sized for the NdFeB parts, usually with a separate fixture for each magnet circuit size.

## Why audio manufacturers magnetize after assembly

- **A clean voice-coil gap.** A magnetized assembly pulls in steel dust and swarf, and particles in the gap are a common cause of rubbing and buzzing. Unmagnetized parts stay clean until the last step.
- **Easier gluing and centring.** Plates and magnet do not jump together while the adhesive is wet, so the gap stays concentric.
- **Simpler handling.** Unmagnetized motor assemblies can be stacked, stored and moved between workstations without sticking to each other or to tooling.

## Setting up a speaker magnetizing station

### Fixture and location

The fixture locates the assembly concentrically and supports it against the forces of the pulse. Complete-driver fixtures also need clearance for the frame and cone, so they are designed from the full speaker drawing.

### Energy and cycle rate

Energy is chosen for the largest and most demanding magnet circuit on the line. Cycle rate then depends on capacitor recharge time and how much heat the fixture can shed between pulses.

### Checking the result

A gaussmeter probe in the gap gives a direct reading of gap flux density that can be compared with a reference speaker, while peak-current monitoring confirms every assembly received a full pulse. With a suitable fixture, the same setup can also handle [re-magnetizing weakened speaker magnets](/applications/magnet-recharging/), as with older alnico drivers.

## What decides the price of a speaker magnetizer?

Each speaker magnetizer is sized for the magnet circuits it will charge, so the price is worked out from your requirement rather than taken from a list. The main cost drivers are:

| Cost driver | How it affects the price |
| --- | --- |
| **Energy rating** | Large ferrite woofer circuits and neodymium drivers need more stored energy than small multimedia speakers. |
| **Voltage** | Higher charging voltage needs capacitors, switching, cables and fixture insulation rated to match. |
| **Fixture design** | Magnet-circuit fixtures are simpler than complete-driver fixtures with clearance for the frame and cone; each speaker size usually needs its own fixture or insert. |
| **Cooling and cycle time** | High-volume lines need faster recharging and forced-air or water cooling so the fixture keeps pace. |
| **Controls and monitoring** | Peak-current monitoring, data logging and a gaussmeter station for gap flux checks add to the basic machine. |
| **Automation** | Conveyor or automatic loading and links to the assembly line add hardware and engineering. |
| **Installation** | Commissioning, operator training and recommended spares are part of the total cost. |

The machine that suits a multimedia speaker line is very different from one for large PA woofers or neodymium tweeters, which is why it pays to specify the most demanding magnet circuit you plan to run.
