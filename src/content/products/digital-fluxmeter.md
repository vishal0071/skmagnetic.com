## How a digital fluxmeter works

A fluxmeter is connected to a **search coil** — a coil of wire that surrounds the part or the space where the part is placed. When the flux through the coil changes, for example when a magnet is pushed in or pulled out, a small voltage appears across the coil. The fluxmeter **integrates** that voltage over time and divides by the number of turns, which gives the change in flux. Because the result depends only on the flux at the start and the end, not on how fast the part is moved, readings are repeatable from one operator to the next.

## Fluxmeter and Helmholtz coil

With a **Helmholtz coil**, a fluxmeter measures the **magnetic moment** of a magnet. Dividing the moment by the magnet's volume gives its magnetization, which can be compared with the values expected for its grade. This is a quick way to check incoming magnets or to compare suppliers without cutting or destroying samples.

## Using a fluxmeter after magnetization

A fluxmeter is the simplest way to prove that a [magnetizer](/products/magnetizer-machines/capacitive-discharge-impulse-magnetizer/) setting is right. Magnetize test parts at increasing charging voltages and measure each one: when the flux stops rising, the part is saturated. In production, measuring parts in a test coil and comparing the result with limits shows straight away if a part was not fully magnetized.
