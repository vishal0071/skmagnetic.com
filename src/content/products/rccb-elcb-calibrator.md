## How an RCCB / ELCB calibrator works

A residual current breaker compares the current flowing out through the live conductor with the current returning through the neutral. If some current leaks to earth — through a fault or a person — the two no longer balance, and the breaker trips. The calibrator recreates that situation in a controlled way: it passes a **set residual current through one pole** of the breaker, then measures the **current at which it trips** and the **time it takes to trip**.

## Tests performed

| Test | What it checks |
| --- | --- |
| **No-trip at ½ × IΔn** | The breaker must not trip at half its rated residual current, so it does not trip without reason in service. |
| **Ramp test** | The test current rises slowly until the breaker trips; the tripping current must lie between ½ × IΔn and IΔn. |
| **Trip time at IΔn** | A general-type RCCB must trip within 300 ms at its rated residual current. |
| **Trip time at 2 × and 5 × IΔn** | Higher leakage must be cleared faster: within 150 ms at 2 × IΔn and 40 ms at 5 × IΔn. |

Limits are for general (non-time-delayed) RCCBs to IEC 61008; time-delayed breakers and other standards have their own values.
